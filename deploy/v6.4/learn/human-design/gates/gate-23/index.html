Gate 23: Assimilation

Insight that needs its own words.

When it overshoots

Explanations nobody asked for.

At its best

Genius phrased so it can be heard.

Carried and open

In a chart that carries it

Carried natally, explaining something original feels urgent; plain words travel further than clever ones.

In a chart where it is open

Absent from the chart, this gate lets a borrowed insight arrive half translated; asking for the simple version is not a weakness.

When it is in the sky

Fresh insight is trying to find words. Simplicity is what makes it understood.

What do you understand that you have not yet found the words for?

The six lines

  1. Line 1, The InvestigatorAn insight needs plain language beneath it, so start by finding the simplest words that will carry it.
  2. Line 2, The HermitUnderstanding lands whole, all at once, and the explanation comes out clearest when somebody has asked for it.
  3. Line 3, The MartyrExplaining an insight badly teaches how to explain it well, one bewildered listener at a time.
  4. Line 4, The OpportunistAn insight travels best through friendships, where people are patient enough to hear it through to the end.
  5. Line 5, The HereticNot every insight has to be useful at once, and a single practical translation of one is a generous offering.
  6. Line 6, The Role ModelOver the years the insights that lasted were the ones other people could repeat in their own words.
