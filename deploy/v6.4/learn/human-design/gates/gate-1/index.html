Gate 1: Self-Expression

Creative individuality that has to come out in its own form.

When it overshoots

Performing for approval.

At its best

Original expression that needed no audience.

Carried and open

In a chart that carries it

Where the gate is already part of the chart, the familiar urge to make something unmistakably personal arrives with more fuel than usual.

In a chart where it is open

With the gate absent from the chart, originality arrives from outside, through what others are making, and can be sampled without being owned.

When it is in the sky

While this gate is lit, the sky favours work made in its own shape. Originality is easier to risk and harder to hide, and imitation feels unusually thin.

What would you make if it did not have to resemble anything that already exists?

The six lines

  1. Line 1, The InvestigatorThe new thing wants roots before it wants an audience; learn the craft underneath the idea and the originality will carry weight.
  2. Line 2, The HermitThe creative gift works best unobserved, and it is usually someone passing by who notices what was made and asks for more.
  3. Line 3, The MartyrOriginality is found by making the wrong version first, and whatever fails in the attempt shows the shape the work actually wants.
  4. Line 4, The OpportunistThe work travels through the people already close, and one friend who truly sees it is worth more than a room of strangers.
  5. Line 5, The HereticOnce the work is public, people decide what it means for them, and the maker’s only job is to keep the form honest.
  6. Line 6, The Role ModelStep far enough back and no single piece is the point; the expression was the whole run of them, the failures included.
