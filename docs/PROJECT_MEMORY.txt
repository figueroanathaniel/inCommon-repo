PROJECT: inCommon v1.4.2
LAST UPDATED: 2026-08-04

VISION
A consent-first self-knowledge app combining astrology, numerology, human design,
and AI-guided reflection. It reads a chart, names its sources, and never claims
to know what will happen.

FRAMEWORK
Design Components (DCLogic). Entry point: "inCommonApp v2.dc.html".
Standalone/offline build: "inCommon Offline Source.dc.html" -> "inCommon (offline).html".
Shell is wrapped in ios-frame.jsx (IOSDevice) for prototype presentation.

ARCHITECTURE
Local-only. localStorage / IndexedDB. Offline-first. Single-file bundled build.
No cloud sync. Service worker: sw.js (cache incommon-v2, cache-first for local
modules, stale-while-revalidate for Google Fonts).
PWA: manifest.json, icon-192.png, icon-512.png, splash.png. Installable on iOS
via Share > Add to Home Screen. Safe-area insets handled on the app shell.

MULTI-PROFILE
Max 6 profiles. Active profile drives every reading. Birth-data consent is
assumed at creation. Memory consent (journal, mood, conversations) is OFF by
default and toggled after creation. PIN-gated switching and deletion.

STELLA
The guide. Epistemic tags are mandatory: [CALCULATED], [TRADITIONAL],
[POSSIBILITY], [SYNTHESIS]. No diagnosis. No destiny declarations. No "I feel"
or "I need you." Safety router is two-layer: deterministic then semantic.

EPISTEMIC RULE
Every interpretation carries a tag. No untagged spiritual or psychological claim
appears anywhere in the app.

VOICE
Scholarly and unhurried. Never the vocabulary of machinery: no calculate,
compute, algorithm, data, process, system, function, output, input, variable,
parameter. Use reveals, shows, speaks, suggests, indicates, points toward,
unfolds, emerges, dwells in, carries the quality of, holds the memory of.
NO EM DASHES anywhere in generated content. Use a period, a comma, or a colon.

TIME
All astrological and numerological reckoning is standardised on Eastern time,
regardless of device location. Display format: "Monday, July 28, 2026 - 11:47 AM
EST". The clock refreshes every 60 seconds (this._clk in componentDidMount).

SABIAN SYMBOLS
360 verified symbols after Marc Edmund Jones (public domain), modified for
modern sensitivity. Lives in Library as the Sabian Lexicon.
Files: sabian-symbols.js, sabian-symbols-data.js

PLACEMENT PAGES
Every planet, asteroid, node, and angle has its own page with ten sections:
House, Sign in House, Planet, Planet in House, Planet in Sign, Dignity
(conditional), Synthesis, Strengths/Challenges/Opportunities/Obstacles, Degree
Analysis, Sabian Symbol. Content engine: placement-content.js.
Decan rulers follow the CHALDEAN sequence (Aries: Mars, Sun, Venus), each with a
ruler "filter" description. This is deliberate. Do not swap it for the
triplicity sequence without saying so.

V1.4.1, TOGETHER, AND AN EVERYDAY TONE (2026-08-04)
Source of truth: inCommonApp v2.dc.html. Both features live in the one build and
render in both shells. No forks, no new files.

1. TOGETHER (SYNASTRY)
   A fifth Spirit view: spiritView 'synastry', route /spirit/together, and a
   fifth entry in VT_TABS so the vertical shell reaches it by swiping past Angel
   Numbers. Desktop reaches it from the My Charts hub, where chartCards gained a
   Together card whose meta line reports how many profiles are shared.
   Gate order, and it matters: no second profile -> "This one takes two"; a
   second profile but no pair consent -> "Two people, two yeses", which links to
   Shared Guidance; consent on both sides -> the reading. Consent is read live
   through ProfileManager.getTwoPersonConsent, never cached, so revoking in
   Settings removes the page on the next render.
   What it draws, all on device from two birth dates:
     - a summary ring, 24 to 94. It is a RATIO of harmonious to frictional
       contact weight, not a running total, so a busy chart cannot score high
       just for being busy. Weight is the PRODUCT of the two bodies' personal
       weights times an orb factor, which is what keeps Sun/Moon/Venus contacts
       above an outer planet that happens to sit close.
     - the six contacts worth naming, each with CALCULATED degrees and orb,
       TRADITIONAL reading, POSSIBILITY caveat. SYN_SWEET flags the six pairs
       people mean by chemistry and marks them "the tender one".
     - a 7x7 aspect grid (Sun through Saturn, rows are the active profile).
       Marked data-chart="synastry" because a grid cannot give 44px to 49 cells.
     - element blend, two bars per element, active profile in --ac and the other
       in --ac2 fill.
     - Life Path pairing, with a single sentence when both numbers match.
     - Human Design electromagnetics: channels where one gate comes from each
       chart and neither person has both. Caveated for missing birth times.
   Voice is deliberately celebratory, addressed to two people at once:
   "Good news, {A} and {B}." The caveat under the ring is load bearing. A number
   is a summary, not a verdict.

2. EVERYDAY TONE
   A fourth reply tone beside Quick, Standard and Deep. Basic conversation is
   answered ON DEVICE and never reaches the model: greeting, farewell, thanks,
   how-are-you, a bare yes/ok, and a mirror case for any short scrap she cannot
   place, where she quotes the user's own words back rather than guessing.
   21 languages: en es pt fr it de nl pl ru tr ar hi zh ja ko sw tl vi id el he.
   Detection is script first (Arabic, Hebrew, Cyrillic, Greek, Devanagari, Kana,
   Hangul, Han), then colloquial markers per language, and it is STICKY: the
   chosen language persists in state.stLang and localStorage
   incommon.stella.lang, so a follow-up in one word stays in the right language.
   Every reply is a greeting plus either a joke or a chart line plus an opening
   question, alternating on state.stTurn so a repeated "hello" does not repeat.
   Chart lines stay tagged CALCULATED and are filled from the user's own Sun
   sign and Life Path. Safety comes first: the router still runs before any of
   this, so a hard message is never answered with small talk.
   chatOut() appends a short language directive to anything that DOES reach the
   live chain, so a chart question asked in Polish comes back in Polish.
   Chat bubbles carry dir="auto" so Arabic and Hebrew set right to left.

3. NEW STATE AND STORAGE
   state: synWith (chosen partner profile id), stLang, stTurn.
   localStorage: incommon.stella.lang. Nothing else was added.

4. AUDIT
   Palette unchanged: no new hexes, #8b5cf6 never used as text, --crisis absent

FIRST RUN

V1.4.3, REAL SIGN-IN WINDOWS, AND SMALL TALK IN EVERY TONE (2026-08-04)

1. SMALL TALK WAS TONE GATED. THAT WAS THE BUG.
   chatterSegs() only ran when the tone was Everyday, so "hi stella" in Standard
   went to the model, came back as a reading, failed the post-processor length
   contract, and the user was shown the draft-rejected message. Greetings,
   farewells, thanks and how-are-you are now recognised in EVERY tone. The two
   looser cases, a bare acknowledgement and the mirror, stay behind Everyday,
   because in Standard a four word message is usually a question. The meta line
   reads "small talk" outside Everyday and "everyday tone" inside it.
   CHAT_GUARD still protects chart questions: "what is my life path" is never
   treated as conversation.

2. OAUTH IS A REAL POPUP AND A REAL HANDSHAKE
   oauthOpen() now does window.open with a named target, listens for a
   postMessage from that window, polls for the user closing it, and cleans both
   up in componentWillUnmount. States: waiting, approved, cancelled, closed
   early, and popup blocked.
   WHY IT STILL CANNOT REACH GOOGLE, META, TIKTOK OR OPENAI: a provider will
   only redirect to an https URI that is whitelisted against a registered client
   id, and the code exchange needs a secret that cannot live in a browser. This
   build has no backend and no registered origin. Additionally OpenAI publishes
   no consumer "sign in with ChatGPT" for third parties, and TikTok Login Kit
   requires app review before any redirect works.
   THE SEAM IS ONE LINE. oauthUrl(id) reads window.INCOMMON_OAUTH[id] =
   {clientId, redirectUri, scope}. With that set, the same popup opens the
   provider's own authorize URL from OAUTH_ENDPOINTS. Without it, the popup
   renders inCommon's own consent screen, which says so in its footer. The
   postMessage contract, {source:'incommon-oauth', provider, ok, email}, does not
   change either way, so the callback page only has to post that message.


V1.4.4, THE VOICE LAYER, RECONCILED (2026-08-05)

1. A VOICE DOCUMENT ARRIVED THAT THE PIPELINE WOULD HAVE REJECTED WHOLESALE
   uploads/stella_voice_reference_v1.4.4.md carries six gold-standard responses
   written for persona. All six would have been WITHHELD by the live pipeline.
   Not for tone. For contracts the document could not see: no epistemic tags,
   em dashes throughout, tradition claims stated as fact without attribution,
   no SOURCES line, and 180 to 260 words against a 180-word ceiling.
   THE LESSON WORTH KEEPING: a voice reference has to be tested AGAINST the
   pipeline, not beside it. Persona work written in isolation encodes invisible
   constraints as hedging. The seven conflicts and their resolutions are in
   handoff/stella-voice-v144-reconciliation.md, which is the reusable artifact.

2. R3 IS THE FIX THAT MATTERS: ATTRIBUTE ONCE, THEN SPEAK
   Name the system one time, then describe declaratively inside that frame.
   "Astrology puts your Sun in the tenth house, and reads Scorpio there as..."
   The tag and the attribution carry the epistemics; the prose does not have to.
   Hedging every clause after the frame is established was making the voice
   mush, and it was never what the contracts asked for. Hedging is not honesty.

3. NEW FILE: stella-voice-v144.js (UMD, no deps, runs in Node)
   voiceSection(mode)   system-prompt block, spliced into buildSystem()
   ARCH                 Reception/Terrain/Reading/Invitation, each mapped onto
                        the healthy loop. A voice surface over an ethical spine,
                        not a rival architecture. Never printed as labels.
   SHOTS                the six examples rewritten contract-compliant
   selectShots(q,n,ctx) domain-matched, capped at TWO per the document's own
                        instruction. Reads chart context as well as the query,
                        because "why do I burn out when I lead" contains no
                        Human Design vocabulary at all and routed to S1 without it.
   checkVoice(text,mode) mechanical drift detector, 14 checks
   TESTS                12 mechanical (self-checking) + 6 judge-only

4. THE READING MODE, AND THE THREE PLACES A MODE HAS TO BE DECLARED
   The document's examples are readings, not chat turns. quick/standard/deep
   were left untouched so the harness kept passing; reading was ADDED:
     stella-prompt-builder.js MODES.reading         t=0.85, max_tokens 1100
     stella-post-processor.js LIMITS.reading        26 sentences, 363 words
                                                    (330 + the 10% grace deep gets)
     stella-api.js            CONFIG.models.reading claude-opus-4-5  (StellaAPI -> 1.1)
   ALL THREE FAIL THE SAME WAY: SILENTLY, BY FALLING BACK TO STANDARD.
   Missing from LIMITS, postProcess caps at 140 words and withholds every
   reading. Missing from CONFIG.models, send() rewrites the mode to standard,
   so the LONGEST mode runs on the mid model tier with the wrong token budget
   and the wrong offline branch. Neither throws. Neither logs.
   A MODE IS NOT WIRED UNTIL IT EXISTS IN ALL THREE FILES. The api.js entry was
   missed on the first pass and only found in review, because no UI calls
   reading yet. Grep the mode name across all three before believing it works.
   Also fixed here: configure() iterated CONFIG.models, so it could only
   overwrite keys that already existed and a host could not patch in a missing
   mode from outside. It now iterates opts.models and accepts new string keys.
   Verified: all six shots pass postProcess in reading mode and all six FAIL it
   as standard, which is how you know the new entry carries them and no gate
   was loosened.

5. SHOTS GO IN THE USER TURN, NEVER THE SYSTEM PROMPT
   buildPrompt gained an optional fifth argument:
     {injectShots, maxShots, chartContext} -> <voice_example> blocks prepended
     to the user message, plus shotsInjected[] on the returned payload.
   WHY NOT THE SYSTEM PROMPT: evaluation-suite E9 counts exactly three
   "<example mode=" blocks in the system text. Adding voice shots there breaks
   it. The document also prescribes user-turn injection on drift. Both agree.
   Soft dependency: stella-prompt-builder.js resolves StellaVoice off the global
   and behaves exactly as V1.0 if the file is absent. It is deliberately NOT in
   modulesReady()'s hard gate.

6. LOAD ORDER
   stella-voice-v144.js goes BEFORE stella-prompt-builder.js in the helmet.

7. THE GATE HELD
   Verification V1.4.dc.html, full run, after wiring: 110 assertions, 0 failed.

   Final bundle probe: api 1.1, builder 1.1, pp 1.0, voice 1.4.4; all four modes

7b. VERIFYING A REBUILT BUNDLE: THE PREVIEW CACHES index.html
   Three consecutive rebuilds appeared to ship a stale stella-api.js. The bundle
   was correct every time; the PREVIEW was serving the first index.html from
   cache, so the probe kept reading old globals. A plain location.reload() did
   not clear it. What works:
     location.href = location.pathname + '?cb=' + Date.now()
   Do that before believing any version number read out of a rebuilt bundle.
   The bundle itself cannot be grepped for the answer, because assets are stored
   compressed and UUID-mapped, so plaintext and naive base64 probes both miss.
   Cost of not knowing this: a filename was churned to bust a cache that was
   never the problem. It has been reverted; stella-api.js keeps its name.

     The tone radio is still quick/standard/deep/everyday. Reading is

     A fifth chip at 375px fails Check A one of two ways: it shrinks under the

     SEQUENCE WHEN IT IS PICKED UP (as V1.4.5 / V1.5.0, its own pass):

--- V1.5.7 verified: 134 of 134, and two false alarms ---------------------
Full V1.4 harness run against the theme build. 134 of 134 passed: the matrix
(A to D plus S), the resize run, the functional sweep including F4c, and the
24 theme rows. No T-TIMEOUT, no T-ERR. First green run of the complete suite

Two diagnoses arrived before the run and both were wrong. Recording them
because each was wrong in a way that will recur.

  FALSE ALARM 1, "the harness lost same-origin access". The evidence was a real
  SecurityError naming claudeusercontent.com, and the conclusion was that the
  preview serves the verification page and the app frame from different origins,
  so contentWindow.document is no longer readable and the harness must be
  rebuilt around postMessage. A probe settled it: parent and child share scheme,
  host and port, document and getComputedStyle are readable across the boundary,
  __incommonApp is reachable, no sandbox attribute. The error came from a blind
  Object.keys(window) scan, which enumerates top and parent, and the HOST page is
  cross-origin. The harness never touches top or parent, only child frames it
  creates. The falsification was available for free: runMatrix, runCross and
  runSweep use the same frame() helper across a hundred passing rows, so an
  origin change cannot break the theme phase alone. Also note the proposed cure
  was impossible: a cross-origin window exposes no named properties either, so
  win.__incommonApp throws exactly like contentWindow.document. postMessage is
  the only survivor, and it would have the app grade itself, which is the one
  thing contrastFails() exists not to do.

  FALSE ALARM 2, mine, "the theme phase has outgrown its 420s budget". Run
  standalone it appeared to take between 361s and 428s, and I proposed widening
  the cap. The real figure is about 10s: __v14ThemeMs, measured in the
  foreground during the full run, read 10283. The 428s was background timer
  throttling. Chrome clamps setTimeout to roughly one call a minute in a
  backgrounded tab, and the six theme cases metered out at exactly one a minute.
  I was polling between sleeps with the tab out of focus and read the clamp as
  render cost.

  The cap is 600s anyway, which is now correct for a different reason: a run
  left in a background tab genuinely would die at 420s, as that one did. The
  comment at the call site states the real cause, because the old rationale
  would have been read later as evidence the theme work was expensive.

  Rule out of this: never time a phase by the wall clock across a poll. Read
  __v14ThemeMs. Any duration measured while the tab was backgrounded is fiction.

  T-ERR EXISTS. I said it did not, having grepped the verification page instead
  of the harness. It is the catch-all row at the tail of runThemes, emitted when
  the phase throws outside a per-case try. It has never fired.

  The b rows now carry their coverage count in the row name, "(saw 132)". The
  count cannot go in expected or actual because mkPush compares by
  JSON.stringify and a passing row has to be equal. A bare green "plausible"
  could not tell 900 elements from 41, and CLAUDE.md already warns that a check
  reporting green means nothing until you confirm it examined a plausible
  number. Now the report says so on its face. The gate itself, checked > 40, is
  unchanged.
