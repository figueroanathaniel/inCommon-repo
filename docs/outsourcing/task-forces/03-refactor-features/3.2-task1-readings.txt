[1] Sleep is the least negotiable teaching in this system, and the one students most often skip. Everything else can be experimented with slowly. This one is nightly. You are a Generator. Go to bed before you are tired and let the last of the day burn off lying down. The tradition repeats this more than any other instruction. The instruction is small and the compliance is difficult, which is generally the mark of a teaching worth keeping.

[2] Your authority shapes this as much as your type does. Your chart gives Sacral Go to bed before the energy runs out, so the last of it discharges lying down. A student will ask how to tell the difference between the authority speaking and the mind speaking. The answer is unsatisfying and correct: the authority arrives without an argument attached. The mind arrives with reasons. When you find yourself building a case, you are already past the moment.

[3] Now the centers that carry this in you, beginning with what is fixed. Your defined Sacral center. The rule the tradition is most insistent about: go to bed before you are tired, and let the last of the energy burn off lying down. Your defined Root center. Adrenal pressure keeps running after the day ends. Winding the body down deliberately does more than any bedtime rule. What is fixed is reliable, and what is reliable is easy to overspend, because it never announces that it is running low.

[4] And what is open, which is where the tradition says most of the trouble and most of the wisdom both live. Your open Head center. Sleeping in your own space matters more than for most. The day’s borrowed questions loosen their hold once the room is yours again. Your open Ajna center. Certainty borrowed during the day comes apart at night. Deciding nothing important after dark is a reasonable rule here. Your open Solar Plexus center. A day spent absorbing other people’s moods needs a real transition before bed, not a screen. An open center is not a deficiency. It is a place designed to sample rather than to hold. The difficulty is that a sample taken often enough starts to feel like a possession, and you begin defending as your own something you only ever borrowed.

[5] Your lines add the rhythm. Line 3, which you are conscious of: A day of trial and error needs an honest review, not a replay in the dark. Fixed circuitry does not soften with effort, and the tradition would rather you arranged a life around it than spent one arguing with it.

[6] Take one thing from this and let the rest wait. Get horizontal before you are finished, and let the end of the day happen lying down rather than sitting up. It is a small instruction and the one the tradition repeats most, which is usually a sign that it was hard-won.

  Strengths: A body that tells you plainly when it has been used enough. | A reliable internal sense of timing. | A mind that keeps its shape, so a routine actually holds.
