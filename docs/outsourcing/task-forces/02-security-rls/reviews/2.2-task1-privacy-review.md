## Question 6, measured: how close an activation list comes to a birth

Measured, not inferred. 30 random birth minutes from 1950 to 2010 UT were
searched against every minute from 1900-01-01 to 2100-12-31: 105,716,160
minutes. The positions, gates and design moment are the app's own code, lifted
(method at the end). The script and its raw output (`handout-anonymity.js`,
`run30.json`) were kept in the review session and are not committed.

| What the handout prints | Minutes in 200 years that share it | Separate runs | Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| 26 activations, body and side labelled, gate.line | 9 to 105, median 62 | always 1 | always the birth year |
| 26 activations, labelled, gate only | 75 to 666, median 340 | always 1 | always the birth year |
| the set of gates, no body, side or line (the shape `hdGatesFor` returns) | 75 to 666, median 349 | 1, twice 2 | always the birth year |
| type, authority, profile, definition | about 50,000 to 1,840,000, median about 690,000 (sampled) | not measured | many |
| the same plus defined centres and channels (6.5's mechanics) | median 1 hit in 150,000 samples, 9 of 30 targets 0 hits; inside the birth year, median 3 hits in 4,000 samples, 5 of 30 targets 0 | not measured | |

Every exact match included the true birth minute, and each one was a single
unbroken stretch on the true day. **A line level list gives the birth date and
the time to within about an hour (median 62 minutes). A gate list, or just the
gates, gives the date and the time to within a few hours (median under six).**
Labels add nothing at gate level, because the slow bodies and the Sun and
Earth pair already fix which gate is which.

The sampled rows are estimates. A zero in 150,000 samples bounds the share at
about 2,100 minutes over the whole span (95%). Inside one year, a zero in 4,000
samples bounds it at about 400 minutes of the 525,600. Whether those minutes
cluster on a few days was not measured.

## Findings, most severe first

### F1. High. Any handout that lists activations, or even the gates, is birth data
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:341-356` (6.5).
The model says a handout carries no birth data unless the reader turns it on,
and flags activations only as an inference. Measured above: a labelled
activation list at gate level, and even the unlabelled set of gates, matches
exactly one run of a few hours on one day in two hundred years. A line level
list narrows it to about an hour. Every match fell in the target's own run, in
the target's own year. Printing that list is giving out the birth date and
the birth time to the hour.
The Privacy Policy already concedes the point for the Oracle
(`app/privacy.html:142`: "the chart positions in the summary are precise
enough that someone could work out approximately when you were born").
**For the 1.2 primary:** 6.5 should class activations, gate lists, lines and
any gate number as birth data, under the same off by default tick as date and
time. The mechanics handout without them is in F1b.

### F1b. Medium. The mechanics-only handout is much weaker, but its full form still singles a birth out
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:344-346`.
Measured above. Type, authority, profile and definition on their own are
shared by about one birth minute in 150 (median), across many years. That
handout does not identify anybody. Add 6.5's defined centres and channels and
the median signature is shared by roughly 400 minutes inside the birth year
(3 hits in 4,000 samples), and some by under 400. To someone who knows the
reader's age, or can guess it, that is a short list of possible birth
moments. How many separate days those minutes cover was not measured.
**For the 1.2 primary and for Task 3:** the default handout should be type,
strategy, authority, profile and definition. Defined centres and channels
should sit behind the reader's own choice, with the reason given, and never be
switched on by default for a client.

### F2. High. Chart versions would sync whatever the "Save my profile" switch says (G2, multiplied)
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:48-50, 382, 466-471`; `app/incommon-cloud.js:160-165, 195-221`.
Today profile rows always sync ("birthData is not a toggle", cloud module
comment at 160-165). O2 (b) ties versions to the profile row, so the same rule
would send **every earlier birth date, time and place** to the server,
including for a reader who switched saving off. The model says G2 is still
open (48-50), but its own order of work (section 10 steps 2 and 3) builds
`chart_versions` and its push before G2 is decided, and nothing in step 3
gates the push.
**For the 1.2 primary:** make it a rule that `chart_versions` gets no push
path until G2 is decided, or that versions are gated on `birthData` consent
explicitly, whatever the profile row does.

### F3. High. Clear birth data (O8) is not guaranteed to clear the history on the other devices
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:394-395, 437-440`; `app/incommon-cloud.js:456-462, 566-574, 620-628`; `tools/run-module-tests.js:2667` (K66).
The model names a new outbox op (`del-chart-versions`) but no merge rule for
pulling versions. Each existing rule brings a cleared version back:
- **Append only**, the natural fit for a history and the rule the Throughline
  follows ("only ever added"): a deletion never reaches the other devices,
  which keep every earlier birth and put it in their exports.
- **Three way, like memories**: K66 says that on a device's first pull, rows
  "the server lacks are sent again, not deleted". Any device on its first pull
  for the account uploads the cleared versions again. A device with an edit
  since its last sync also keeps its copy ("changed here since: keep it",
  620-628).
- The merge's outbox scan (572-574) knows only `del-profile`, `del-memory` and
  `del-memories`, so a pending `del-chart-versions` is not protected the way
  K67 protects the others.
**For the 1.2 primary:** specify a clear marker that a pull honours on every
device, a first pull included (for example `versions_cleared_at` on the
profile row, with every version older than it deleted on arrival and refused
on push). The marker has to be a timestamp, not birth data. Also add
`del-chart-versions` to the merge's pending set, plus rows that drive a second
device and a first pull device against a clear, like K65 to K67.

### F4. High. Note snapshots (`resolved`) are derived birth data outside every birth data gate
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:127-140, 61, 381, 345`.
`resolved` holds activation values (`{ value: 'gate.line' }`), gate lines and
the `by:` lists of activation addresses. By F1, enough of them together
reconstruct the birth. Under the model they:
- sync under `journal` (O1), not under whatever governs birth data, so they
  reach the server for a reader whose birth data does not;
- survive Clear birth data, because O8 keeps notes, so the button removes the
  versions and leaves the derived values of every one of them;
- can ride into a handout as "whichever notes the reader picks" (345) and into
  `incommon.charts.v1` with `includes.notes`.
**For the 1.2 primary:** treat `resolved` as birth data. On Clear birth data,
strip it and keep the text and the address. Keep it out of handouts, and out
of any export where `includes.birth_data` is false. Either it does not travel
in the synced memory `content`, or it travels only where the version it came
from may travel.

### F5. Medium. A `chart_note` memory fails open on consent unless MEMORY_CONSENT learns it
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:381, 390-392, 470-471`; `app/profile-manager.js:525-527, 580`; `app/incommon-cloud.js:172-180`.
`consentFor(type)` returns `MEMORY_CONSENT[type] || null`, and `memoryAllowed`
returns true when the consent it needs is null. A `chart_note` row whose
`consent_required` is null (the Archive restore case K71 was written for)
would therefore sync with journal off, and the view filter at 580 would show
it while journal is off. The model's step 3 changes `SYNC_MEMORY_TYPES` and K69
but not `MEMORY_CONSENT`.
**For the 1.2 primary:** add `chart_note: 'journal'` to `MEMORY_CONSENT` in the
same change, with a K row that restores a note with null consent and journal
off and expects nothing to go up.

### F6. Medium. Import can launder consent
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:270-272` against `336-337`.
Rule 8 of 6.1 says imported people arrive with `basis: none` "whatever the
file says". 6.4 says importing `incommon.charts.v1` is "6.1 with every field
kept, notes included". If 6.4 wins, a hand edited file, or one from another
reader, arrives with `basis: stated`, a `notes` scope and notes switched on.
That breaks people-library.js rule 1 (`app/people-library.js:19-21`: the only
writer writes false).
**For the 1.2 primary:** every import resets `subject_consent` to `none` and
`notes_enabled` to false. Notes for a person come in only after the reader
switches them on for that person during the import, which records the
reader's statement at that moment. The reader's own `self` subject can keep
its notes.

### F7. Medium. `basis: card` overstates what a card proves; `stated` needs a label of who stated it
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:227, 232-237, 450-451`; `app/inCommonApp v2.dc.html:23307-23314, 23508`.
The model defines `card` as "the person sent it". `cardOf()` builds the card
from the active profile, and a profile can be somebody else's (finding F9), so
a card proves only that its sender held the details. Question 5 (`stated`
never shown as proof) has nothing built to check yet. The one place it can
fail by design is the export: a `subject_consent` with `basis: stated`, read
by someone outside the app, looks like a consent record.
**For the 1.2 primary:** define `card` as "arrived on a card", and do not let
`card` on its own open the `notes` or `handout` scopes. In the export, write
`subject_consent` as what the reader recorded (for example
`recorded_by: "reader"`).

### F8. Medium. Withdrawal removes the notes and keeps the birth data
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:230`.
`withdrawn_at` clears only `notes` from scope. A client who withdraws has
withdrawn `store` as well, yet their birth record and every version stay on
the device.
**For the 1.2 primary:** a withdrawal that takes in `store` deletes the record
in one write (rule 2), or the screen asks the reader which they mean.
Handouts already sent cannot be recalled, so the consent capture for
`handout` should say so (that wording belongs to Task 3).

### F9. Medium. A profile made for another person syncs, and O2 adds its history (question 4)
`app/incommon-cloud.js:160-165, 195-221`; `app/inCommonApp v2.dc.html:25532-25534, 23508`; `app/privacy.html:124, 208`; `HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:166-169, 445-449`.
Confirmed in the code. A profile made for a partner or a child goes to
`profiles` unconditionally. As the active profile it is what the Oracle
dossier is built from, so its chart summary goes to the model provider, and
what the card carries. Privacy §5 calls these "your profiles"; §14 allows
profiles for others, with permission. The model's own principle (3.3: "they
cannot consent for anybody else") is broken here already, and O2 would add
that person's whole birth history to the server.
**This is the owner's decision.** It is raised here, not resolved. The options
I can see: a "this profile is somebody else" mark that keeps the profile, its
versions and its notes on the device; or plain words in §5 and §14 saying
such a profile and its history are copied to the account.

### F10. Medium. `includes.birth_data: false` is not something the shape can honour; `chart_hash` is a birth data oracle
`HD-STUDY-MODEL.md:175-183, 320-329`; `app/people-library.js:208-213`.
Every version in an export carries the full `birth` record and its
`chart_hash`, so a file marked `birth_data: false` would still hold the birth
unless the versions are removed or stripped as well. `chart_hash` is an
unsalted SHA-256 of the instant (to the minute), whether the time is known,
and latitude and longitude to four places. Anybody who knows the birthday can
test 1,440 minutes against a gazetteer of about 170,000 places, around 2.4
times 10^8 hashes, which a laptop does quickly. The same person also hashes
the same in every reader's file, so two files can be linked.
**For the 1.2 primary:** where birth data is off, versions carry neither
`birth` nor `chart_hash`. `chart_hash` never appears in a handout.

## Method and reproduction

`handout-anonymity.js`, kept in the review session and not committed. It lifts `lonRaw`, `norm360`,
`meanLunarNodeOf`, `PL_EL`, `RAD`, `ASTEROIDS` and `HD_CHANNELS` out of the
app the way `tools/check-astral-bodies.js` does, and uses the vendored
astronomy-engine 2.1.19, `hd-wheel.js` `gateLine`, `arc-solver.js`
`designMoment` and `lunar-mean-points.js`. It computes thirteen activations a
side, as `hdChart()` does after D-01.

Targets are random whole minutes in 1950 to 2010 UT (seed 777). For each one,
every year from 1900 to 2100 is searched in the window where the personality
Sun holds the target's gate (or line), and each minute in it is compared on
all 26 activations. Years whose slow bodies (Mars outward, and the node) sit on
one non-matching value at every half day sample are skipped. That prefilter
was checked with it switched off on one target, scanning all 201 years: same
counts (194 and 27 minutes).

The unlabelled gate set searches every Sun gate whose opposite gate is also in
the set. The mechanics signature is type, authority (Ego split), profile,
definition parts, defined centres and defined channels. It is estimated by
sampling random minutes over 1900 to 2100, and within each target's own year.

Positions are geocentric and the time is UT. The place does not enter Human
Design, which is why no place is searched.

### Logic gaps, most severe first

1. **"No birth date on the server" now means two things.** Section 4 rule 5
   (b) and (c), lines 223 to 231, read an empty birth on the server as a
   clear. Since F2 (`incommon-cloud.js:197`, `birthSaved` and the empty
   push), an empty birth also means saving is off. Section 13, line 654,
   settles the clash by letting Clear birth data with saving off clear only
   the device it was pressed on. Another device then keeps the birth and the
   whole history, and turning saving on there uploads the birth the reader
   cleared, so O8 fails across devices. Suggested: a clear marker that carries
   no birth data and travels in the consent ledger, which already syncs
   whatever the switch says, honoured by every pull, a first pull included.
2. **The `for_other` mark never leaves the device that set it.** `forOther()`
   (`incommon-cloud.js:216`) is checked only on that device. The row is not
   sent, so other devices never learn of the mark. A device that changed the
   profile since its last sync (line 694), is on its first pull (line 693),
   or runs an older build pushes the profile back as an ordinary one. Because
   `del-profile` is queued once, on the mark (line 1908), the other person's
   birth details then stay on the server. Suggested: carry the mark itself,
   as a ledger event or a tombstone.
3. **`share-rules.js` has no kind for a profile made for somebody else.**
   `KINDS` (line 67) is self, person and client, so a marked profile would be
   shaped as `self`: first name on (line 113), and centres, gates, activations
   and birth date with no word (line 114). Suggested: map it to `person`, with
   a T row.
4. **The switch is enforced only by up to date clients.** 0005 (line 44)
   checks ownership only, and `profiles` takes any birth a client sends. An
   older cached build on a second device keeps uploading the birth, and the
   new pull keeps its local copy rather than removing the server's.
   `privacy.html:125` promises "the next time the app syncs it removes them".
   For the owner: a server rule, or softer wording.
5. **Marking a profile as someone else's deletes its server row and not its
   Oracle readings**, because `oracle_readings` is keyed by user rather than
   profile.
