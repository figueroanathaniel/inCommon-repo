# Jovian Archive

The rights holder for Ra Uru Hu's Human Design material and for the marks that
name it. Founded 1999. Registered as Jovian Archive Media Pte. Ltd.

They matter to this project in two separate ways, and the two should never be
argued together:

- **As a legal boundary.** They own the marks and the corpus. That boundary is
  hard, checkable, and does not move with the market. See `trademarks.md`.
- **As an incumbent.** They ship a consumer app and publish a roadmap. That is a
  fact about a competitor and is only useful for as long as it stays current.

## The one-line version

They are giving the product away and pushing revenue to a certified-analyst
marketplace several phases out. Their differentiation is canonical authority
over a corpus they own. inCommon cannot compete on that axis and should not
try: there is no lawful route to an equivalent corpus, and an imitation would be
both weaker and riskier than the thing it imitated.

## The standing constraint that comes out of all of it

inCommon computes the same public facts anybody may compute (ephemeris
positions, the I Ching mapping, the centre topology) and writes every word of
interpretation itself. Facts are not copyrightable and a system is not
copyrightable, 17 U.S.C. section 102(b). Expression is. The line between those
two sentences is the whole of the legal position, and it is the same line the
truth-layer separation already draws for safety reasons: calculated fact,
the reader's own words, traditional interpretation, and synthesis, kept apart.

Enforce it once and it serves both purposes.
