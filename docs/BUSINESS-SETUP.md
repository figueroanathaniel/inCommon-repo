# Registering the business name in Florida

Added 12 October 2026. inCommon is run by Nathaniel J. Figueroa as an
individual in Florida (Terms section 1). Doing business under a name that is not
your own legal name, here "inCommon", requires registering it as a
**fictitious name** under Florida's Fictitious Name Act, section 865.09 of the
Florida Statutes. This is the owner's half; nothing in the code depends on it.

Fees and rules below were checked against Sunbiz's own pages on 12 October 2026.
Check them again on the day you file.

## The links

- Sunbiz, the Division of Corporations: https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/
- Check the name is free first: https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/search/ (choose Fictitious Name);
  how the search works: https://dos.fl.gov/sunbiz/search/guides/fictitious-name/
- File online (choose Fictitious Name Registration): https://efile.sunbiz.org/onlmenu.html
- Fictitious name questions and answers: https://efile.sunbiz.org/ficfaq.html
- The paper form, if you would rather mail it: https://form.sunbiz.org/pdf/cr4e001.pdf

## The steps, in order

1. **Search the name** on Sunbiz. A fictitious name registration does not give
   you exclusive rights to it (a trademark does that), but it is worth knowing
   if someone already uses it.
2. **Advertise the name in a newspaper, once, before you file.** The application
   asks you to certify that you have, so the notice runs first.
3. **File the registration** on Sunbiz. The fee is **$50**, paid by card. It is
   not refundable.
4. **Keep the newspaper's proof of publication** (usually an affidavit they send
   you). Sunbiz does not ask for it, but it is your record that you complied.
5. **Renew every five years.** The registration expires on 31 December of the
   fifth year; Sunbiz mails a renewal notice about three months before.

## Getting the notice published in a local paper

The notice has to run in a newspaper **in the county where the business's
principal place of business is**, and the paper has to qualify to print legal
notices under Chapter 50 of the Florida Statutes. Most county papers do.

1. **Find the paper.** Search "legal notices" with your county's name, or look
   at https://floridapublicnotices.com, the Florida Press Association's site,
   which lists public notices by county and the papers that print them. The
   county clerk's office can also tell you which papers qualify.
2. **Contact its legal advertising desk** (it may be called "legals" or "public
   notices") by phone or email and say you need to publish a **fictitious name
   notice**. Many papers have a form for it and fill in the wording themselves.
3. **Give them the text.** The usual wording is:

   > NOTICE UNDER FICTITIOUS NAME LAW PURSUANT TO SECTION 865.09, FLORIDA
   > STATUTES. NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN that the undersigned, desiring to engage
   > in business under the fictitious name of **inCommon**, located at
   > **[street address]**, in the County of **[county]**, in the City of
   > **[city]**, Florida **[ZIP]**, intends to register the said name with the
   > Division of Corporations of the Florida Department of State, Tallahassee,
   > Florida. Dated at **[city]**, Florida, this **[day]** day of **[month]**,
   > **[year]**. **Nathaniel J. Figueroa**

4. **Pay for one insertion.** It is usually somewhere between $10 and $100.
5. **Ask for the affidavit of publication** and keep it with the business's
   records. Once the notice has run, file on Sunbiz (step 3 above).

**The address becomes public**, in the paper and on Sunbiz. If you work from
home and would rather it were not your home address, ask an accountant or lawyer
whether a business address (a virtual office or registered agent) can serve as
the principal place of business for your situation before the notice runs.

## If the LLC is coming soon

`docs/LEGAL-SETUP.md` plans for inCommon to move into an LLC. An LLC is
registered on Sunbiz too (Articles of Organization), and a registered LLC
trading under its exact registered name needs no fictitious name. It does need
one to trade as plain "inCommon" if its registered name is, say, "inCommon
LLC", so the newspaper step comes back either way.

If the LLC is only weeks away, it may be simpler to form it first and register
the fictitious name once, under the LLC. An LLC is also what lets the iPhone app
be published under the company's name instead of yours: Apple asks an
organisation for a D-U-N-S number (`docs/APP-STORE-SETUP.md`).

## The rest of the paperwork, briefly

- **A local business tax receipt.** Most Florida counties, and many cities,
  require one for a business run from home. Search your county's name with "local
  business tax receipt". It is usually a small annual fee.
- **An EIN** from the IRS (free, at irs.gov). Not required for a sole
  proprietor without employees, but it lets you give Stripe, Apple and Google a
  business number instead of your Social Security number.
- **Income tax.** Luminary income goes on Schedule C, with self employment tax,
  and quarterly estimated payments once you owe more than a small amount. Stripe
  sends a 1099-K. An accountant is worth an hour here.
- **Sales tax** is in `docs/LUMINARY-EU-UK.md`, under "Taxes elsewhere".
